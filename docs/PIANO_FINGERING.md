# Piano fingering knowledge base

Research reviewed 7 November 2026. The resulting applied revision is now documented in [experiment 011](../experiments/011-guarnieri-transition-fingering/README.md); the comparison table below records the earlier research proposals. Application: an intermediate pianist with a comfortable ninth, learning Guarnieri's Ponteio No. 15. Finger numbers are 1 thumb through 5 little finger. Chord lists below run from lowest to highest pitch; C4 is middle C.

The earlier practice edition used 5 for the bass and 4-3-1 for many upper chords too routinely. A comfortable thumb-to-little-finger ninth does not establish the reach of 5-4, or the comfort of a four-note shape. Fingerings should be chosen for the complete hand position and its transitions, including any bass note that remains held. The specific alternatives below are editorial candidates for a keyboard trial, not fingerings endorsed by the cited authors for this piece.

## Working principles

The following procedure is our synthesis of the sources and the score, not a quotation or a published universal rule set.

1. **Assemble everything the hand must do at that instant.** Combine bass and upper voices. Distinguish an attack, a tied continuation and a note sustained only by pedal. Staff placement alone does not settle hand allocation.
2. **Check the internal gaps.** Look at every pair, particularly 5-4 and 4-3. For this review, a 5-4 interval of a major third or more triggers examination; it is not an anatomical limit. A third can work for some hands, and a smaller interval can still be awkward on mixed keys.
3. **Compare complete shapes.** For an LH four-note chord, test 5-3-2-1 against 5-4-2-1 and 5-4-3-1 where appropriate. Do not merely move difficulty to another pair. In LH 5-3-2-1, a wide gap between the middle notes falls on adjacent fingers 3 and 2.
4. **Account for key depth and sound.** Black keys sit farther back. Check how the thumb reaches, how the longer fingers fit, which voice needs emphasis, and whether the wrist can remain free. No single fingering is best for all transpositions of an interval pattern.
5. **Check at least one event before and after.** Keep a necessary held finger available; check repeated-note releases, finger substitutions and changes of position. A comfortable isolated chord can be an awkward departure point.
6. **For unreachable combinations, choose a realization explicitly.** Consider taking an upper note with the other hand when it is available. If a roll or pedal-assisted release is needed, identify when and why. Releasing the bass after its attack does not itself solve an unreachable simultaneous attack. Avoid prescribing continuous pedal through changing chromatic harmony.
7. **Preserve useful consistency after testing.** Use the same solution for genuinely equivalent reprise passages. Keep a different fingering when the bass, key colours, articulation or next event changes.
8. **Treat discomfort as feedback.** Compare candidates gently, with the bass held as intended. Do not repeatedly force a shape to make an editorial digit work. Select the version that remains manageable through the surrounding phrase, then practise it consistently.

## Places where finger numbers alone are insufficient

- Bars 2 and 37, slot 7: D3/A3/B3/G4 spans a perfect eleventh and assigns 5 to both D3 and A3. This cannot describe a single simultaneously held LH position. Earlier prose proposed pedal/rolling, but the music-only PDF does not make that execution clear.
- Bar 12 slot 7 and bars 24-28 slot 7 also reuse 5 for different sounding notes. They require a stated release/redistribution decision, not an implied static grip.
- Bar 30 slot 4 combines held G3 on 5 with newly attacked Gb3 on 4 below it. A conventional static LH ordering is inverted. Resolving the sustain/hand division matters more than changing one digit. This is a fingering/realization flag, not a new transcription correction.
- Bars 32-35 place the upper chords progressively far from a sustained bass. For example, bar 32 slot 7 combines Bb2 with Bb3/Eb4/Ab4, a 22-semitone span. A comfortable ninth cannot encompass it. Pedal timing must be worked out against the actual harmony.
- Bars 51-52 have bass sonorities wider than a ninth before adding later upper events. The old assignments rely on rolling and relocation. These need an explicit performance plan.

## Acceptance checklist for future score revisions

For each changed event, record the pitches, current/candidate fingers, held notes, neighbouring events, hand division, pedal assumptions, source digit status and user feedback. Label a candidate as proposed until tried. Separate a fingering choice from a change in note timing or hand allocation.

After selecting candidates, update repeated passages, preserve black source digits separately from editorial additions, render with the accepted large numeral font, inspect all pages, and compare MIDI with the clean master. Identical MIDI verifies preservation of encoded music; it does not verify physical comfort. If a source digit's attachment is uncertain, resolve or annotate that uncertainty rather than silently treating it as a mandatory finger.
